How to Finish College Applications Over Winter Break (and Handle Early Results)

For many seniors, the hardest part of applying to college isn't the first application. It's the stretch from November to January. Early decision and early action applications are in, results start arriving in December, and regular decision applications still need supplements, recommendations and scores. It all lands during finals, holidays and family travel.

You don't need more willpower to get through it. You need a list, a calendar, and a plan for results day. Here's how to set them up.

Step 1: List every remaining deadline in one place

Start by writing down every application you still plan to submit, with:

  • The plan type (regular decision, ED II, rolling, honors college or scholarship program)
  • The exact deadline, including the time and time zone if the school lists one
  • How many supplemental essays or short answers it needs
  • The application fee, or whether you qualify for a fee waiver
  • Whether it wants test scores, and whether you send them yourself or self-report
  • Whether your recommendations and school report are already submitted

Then sort the list by date and work from the top. Many regular decision deadlines fall between January 1 and February 1, but some schools, honors programs and merit scholarships set earlier dates in November or December. Don't assume. Check each school's admissions website, because that's the authority, not a ranking site or a friend's spreadsheet.

Step 2: Plan the weeks backward

Count the weeks you have left before your last deadline, then give each week one main goal. A typical eight-week plan might look like this:

  1. Finish the deadline list and research questions for each school
  2. Draft all "Why us?" essays
  3. Draft the remaining supplements (community, activity, short answers)
  4. Revise and get one round of feedback
  5. Submit anything due in early January, before the holiday week
  6. Final edits for the remaining schools
  7. Submit the rest, at least a few days early
  8. Check every portal and fix missing items

Leave the last week before each deadline for proofreading and submitting, not writing. Block out days you already know are lost: exams, travel, family events. A plan that ignores those dates won't survive December.

Step 3: Write supplements in batches

Many supplements ask similar questions in different words. Group them by type:

  • Why us? Why this college or this major
  • Community: a group you belong to, or how you'd contribute
  • Activity: expand on one activity or interest
  • Short answers: lists, favorite books, quick questions

Draft one strong answer for each type, then adapt it honestly for each school. Our guide to reusing scholarship essays explains how to reuse material without sounding copied, and the same rules apply here. Our College Essay Workbook covers brainstorming and revision.

The exception is "Why us?" essays. Every one needs details that only fit that school: a specific program, class, professor's research area, student group or opportunity, and why it matters to you. An answer that would still make sense with another college's name in it isn't specific enough. Keep a short research sheet per school with notes from its website, virtual tours and info sessions, so you aren't researching and writing at midnight on the same night.

Step 4: Check everything before you hit submit

Websites slow down and help desks get busy on deadline nights. Aim to submit at least three to five days early. Before you do, check:

  • The school's name is correct in every essay (the classic copy-paste mistake)
  • Every prompt is answered, and word limits are respected
  • Your activities list and honors are complete and consistent
  • Test score, recommendation and school report steps are done or requested
  • You chose the right plan type and the right term
  • The fee is paid or the waiver is approved
  • You saved a PDF of the submitted application and the confirmation email

Ask your counselor about the mid-year report. Many colleges ask for your first-semester senior grades, and your counselor usually sends it, so make sure they know where you applied.

Step 5: Decide your next steps before early results arrive

Results day is easier when you've already decided what each outcome means. Write your plan down before December.

If you're admitted early decision: ED is binding. Follow that school's instructions to accept, pay any deposit by its date, and withdraw your other applications as it asks. If finances are a concern, talk to the school's financial aid office before results day, so you know how they handle that situation.

If you're admitted early action or rolling: Congratulations. Usually you don't have to decide yet. Many U.S. colleges use May 1 as the reply deadline for regular decision admits, but check the school's own date. Keep working on your other applications. An early acceptance is a safety net, not a reason to stop.

If you're deferred: You'll be reconsidered with the regular decision pool. Read the letter carefully, because it usually says exactly what the school wants. Some colleges welcome an update, others ask you not to send anything extra. Make sure your mid-year grades are strong, and keep submitting your regular decision applications.

If you're denied: It hurts, and it's okay to take a day. Then move on to your other applications. You still have options, and many students end up happy at a school that wasn't their first choice in December.

Step 6: Write a letter of continued interest (if the school allows it)

If you're deferred and the school accepts updates, a short letter of continued interest can show you're still serious. Keep it to one page, usually about 250-400 words:

  1. Opening: thank them, and say clearly that the school is still your first choice, but only if that's true
  2. What's new: grades, awards, leadership roles, projects, or scores since you applied
  3. Fit: one or two specific reasons the school fits, ideally new ones
  4. Closing: what you'd contribute, your thanks, and your full name, date of birth or applicant ID

Don't repeat your application, don't plead, and don't send several letters. Send it the way the school asks (often a portal upload), and tell your counselor. Sometimes they can add an update too.

Step 7: Watch the portals

After you submit, most colleges send login details for an applicant portal. Log in to each one and check it every week. Missing-document notices often show up there before anyone emails you. Track each school's status, decision, financial aid offer and reply-by date in one place.
